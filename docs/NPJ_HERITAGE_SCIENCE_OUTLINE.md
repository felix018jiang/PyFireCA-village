# npj Heritage Science 投稿工作稿｜侗寨木构遗产与聚落的防火线设计

## 投稿判断与文章主线

**论文关注仍有人居住的侗寨如何防火并延续其文化空间。** 鼓楼和风雨桥承载集会、交往与通行，周围的民居和街巷维系这些场所的日常使用。防火线划在哪里，会同时影响火势如何穿过聚落、居民需要让出哪些空间、哪些文化活动场所能继续使用。文章从保护鼓楼和风雨桥出发，把居民和他们所生活的聚落纳入保护目标。［1］［[IC-1、IC-2、U-1、U-2](#institutional-sources)］

**技术贡献是用同源、同风的完整火情检验防火线对遗产和聚落的双重影响。** 对每个固定布局，追踪指定遗产有无引燃，分别量化施工清除的普通屋面和火灾烧尽的剩余屋面。两村精简布局分别节省 66.39% 和 77.94% 的模拟清理面积，遗产引燃和民居火损随之增加。把清除与火损合并后，三个分层的普通屋面总损失下降，岜团近源层上升。这组差异使讨论能够落到具体选址，明确一套布局涉及哪些清理位置、保护了哪些指定构筑物，以及哪些火情仍造成遗产引燃或民居火损。［Figs. 4–5 · Table 2］

Discussion 将用这些结果回答防火线如何设置。**居民生命安全设定底线，侗族文化遗产及其日常用途的延续定义保护目标，土地权属与使用关系决定布线的可实施条件。** 模型比较布局的火势传播与物理清理代价。居民、权利人、消防与遗产专业人员共同核实场所用途、用地影响和疏散救援条件，再用模型结果比较满足这些条件的候选。［[U-2、DR-1、CN-1、CN-2、CN-3](#institutional-sources)］

## Results｜4 小节

### Clearance position alters local fire exposure

**R1｜几何位置的近等面积控制。** 交代用已知起火点完成的短时机制对照，基线、既有选址、近等面积的换位方案。岜团两方案均有三个普通屋面标签，清除面积 443.84 与 443.72 m²。平岩均有五个标签，985.61 与 974.45 m²。两个屋面离散尺度、静风、20/40/60 min 检查点。地图应显示所清屋面相对于火点、鼓楼和风雨桥的位置。［Fig. 2 · Supplementary Fig. 2 · Supplementary Table 2］

**R2｜观察到位置效应及案例边界。** 岜团既有选址的指定遗产在这两种起火情形中至 60 min 保持未引燃。换位方案下，鼓楼侧火情中鼓楼首燃约 857 和 906 s，桥侧火情中风雨桥首燃约 1,249 和 1,409 s，细化网格分别为 1 和 0.5 m。用桥侧案例说明 20 min 时仍可能未见引燃。平岩两个换位方案均通过两种短时火情。以场景内对照说明几何与传播路径关系，在后续小节检验它能否扩展到独立火点和终态。［Fig. 2 · [逐点原始结果](results/heritage-followup-20261103/core-checkpoints-dual-scale.csv) · [布局面积](results/heritage-followup-20261103/intervention-areas.csv)］

### Multipoint wide protection reduces terminal ignition

**R3｜60 分钟与熄灭给出不同的风险图景。** 103 处留出火点，每处九种稳态风，四种围护及无清理共五种布局，4,635 场。60 min 时有 4,020 场仍在燃烧，715 场到 60 min 后才第一次有指定遗产引燃。把“场次首次引燃”作为主数字。需要逐座建筑时，在补充数据列出 2,033 次 60 min 后的新增构筑物引燃，同场多个建筑可分别计入。［Fig. 3 · Supplementary Table 4］

**R4｜在相同带宽下比较围护对象。** 顺序先报告终态五布局的总体观测计数，无清理 556/927，遗产窄 548/927，多点窄 486/927，遗产宽 41/927，多点宽 0/927。该计数便于阅读样本，推断范围为本次测试条件。再展示关键的同带宽配对，由遗产宽至多点宽，平岩近 13→0、远 0→0，岜团近 18→0、远 10→0。平岩近、远 n 分别为 207、270，岜团为 180、270。加设围护锚点增加清理 4,237.55 m² 与 2,165.76 m²。围护对象和施工覆盖随之共同变化，此比较估计两种完整策略之差。［Fig. 3 · Table 1 · Supplementary Fig. 3］

**R5｜扩围同时改变聚落损失。** 四层的最终屋面和地表烧尽均值均下降，给出一对直观数字，例如岜团近层全部屋面终态烧尽 31,483.56→28,927.80 m²，地表 408,788.61→380,867.50 m²。宽围护的物理空间代价继续由 Table 1 分项报告。过渡到问题，能否用更少清理保住这一级别的遗产表现。［Table 1 · Supplementary Table 4 · [全分层结果](../output/heritage-wind-validation-20261112/by-stratum.csv)］

### Clearance savings and terminal heritage ignition in compact layouts

**R6｜实际几何和清理量。** 图中并排画同一村两套几何，明确单村共用范围、比例及剖分。平岩 10,669.28→3,585.57 m²，省 7,083.71 m²（66.39%），分别显示普通屋面 4,594.28→2,835.57 m²、地表 6,075→750 m²。岜团 4,937.24→1,089.02 m²，省 3,848.22 m²（77.94%），屋面 2,737.24→1,089.02 m²、地表 2,200→0 m²。指出精简几何在旧火点得到并冻结，留出测试共 927 组，其中宽围护成员来自上一面板。［Fig. 4 · Table 1 · Supplementary Table 3］

**R7｜四层与逐处遗产。** 多点宽围护终态始终 0/927。固定精简共 352/927，按平岩近、远及岜团近、远报 123/207、33/270、103/180、93/270，其中 266 场在 60 min 后才第一次引燃遗产。平岩 B0036 风雨桥引燃 156 场，B0166 鼓楼 7 场，B0035 风雨桥保持未引燃但有非零模拟热暴露。岜团 B0073 鼓楼 146 场、B0120 风雨桥 51 场，两者同场 1 次。用逐处数据说明两村的约束构筑物各异，场次和构筑物数分开报告。可举平岩近源 near-006 东风 4 m/s 同源复算，精简布局的 B0036 约 3 h 46 min 才引燃。［Fig. 5a · Table 2 · Supplementary Fig. 7 · Supplementary Table 5 · [逐处汇总](../output/heritage-geometry-validation-20261118/by-heritage.csv)］

### Accounting for clearance changes the settlement-level ranking

**R8｜只算火损时。** 对比精简减多点宽的普通屋面终态烧尽均值，平岩近 +856.50 m²、远 +302.18 m²、岜团近 +2,032.87 m²、远 +753.11 m²。927 对中 518 对增加、409 对在 0.00001 m² 数值容差内持平。四层最坏场景的民居烧尽也都增加。可报告地表终态烧尽均值四层均上升，数值放补表。屋面和地表分开，最坏值为各布局在本层内的最大观测场景。［Fig. 5b · Table 2 · Supplementary Table 5］

**R9｜把施工和火灾一起算。** 普通屋面总物理损失 = 已清除屋面并集 + 残余屋面烧尽。按同一顺序报配对均值差 −902.21、−1,456.53、+384.65、−895.11 m²，呈现三层平均少失与岜团近源多失。精简布局虽在三层的这项面积指标上占优，四层均出现指定遗产引燃。以联合终态收尾，观察到的折衷指向需同时满足的两个保护条件。［Fig. 5b · Table 2 · [927 对逐场数据](../output/heritage-geometry-validation-20261118/paired-cases.csv)］

## Methods｜English draft

### Study sites and heritage inventory

We studied Pingyan (平岩) and Batuan (岜团), two traditional Dong settlements in Sanjiang, Guangxi, China. The sites offer two spatial arrangements of village roofs and communal timber structures. The analytical units were georeferenced roof polygons digitized from RGB imagery [source and acquisition dates to be supplied]. We retained 950 roof labels in Pingyan and 333 in Batuan. The designated targets were one drum tower (B0166) and two wind-and-rain bridges (B0035 and B0036) in Pingyan, and one drum tower (B0073) and one wind-and-rain bridge (B0120) in Batuan. The remaining 947 and 331 labels, respectively, were assigned to the ordinary-roof class. These assignments describe the study inventory. Footprints provide a measurable representation of the built setting surrounding the communal structures (Fig. 1).

Geometry and area calculations used WGS 84/UTM zone 49N (EPSG:32649). The parent grid was 5 m, with 473 × 598 rows and columns and 275,492 active domain cells in Pingyan, and 312 × 347 rows and columns and 107,606 active cells in Batuan. These domains enclose 6.8873 and 2.69015 km², respectively, as measured on the original model masks. Elevation was taken from tile N25E109 of FABDEM V1-2, at native one-arc-second spacing, and bilinearly resampled onto the parent grid (Supplementary Fig. 1). The terrain raster supplied the base elevation at the model nodes. Image-derived ground layers assigned equivalent fuel to open vegetation, including grass and cultivated land, and to woody vegetation, including trees and shrubs. The remaining ground categories were treated as non-combustible. We used one effective roof-fuel class across ordinary and designated polygons. Supplementary Table 1 gives the corresponding physical inputs and distinguishes nominal class parameters from their discretized representations.［18, 19］

### Roof representation and clearance

Inventory polygons were partitioned into disjoint physical patches with associated roof labels. In Pingyan, the registered heritage-priority convention assigned overlaps with a designated roof to that roof. In Batuan, the input rules required the designated and ordinary labels to pass the overlap checks without such reassignment. A shared ordinary patch retained its fuel when any of its owners was retained. We computed construction clearance as the sum of the union of physically removed ordinary roof area and the full 25 m² footprint of each selected combustible ground cell coded as non-roof in the parent grid. Land-cover fractions determined combustible area within a cell when reporting ground burned out. Ordinary-roof tags identify geometry and affected labels. Area measurements use physical patches, counted once each.

The main wind simulations retained the background grid and locally refined the prescribed source roof and designated roofs to 1 m. The refined polygons were integrated with an area quadrature spacing of 0.5 m. Other nodes used a representative point on the parent support. For the local position comparison, we refined the union of roofs selected by the three alternatives, the source and the designated roofs, and repeated the calculations with 1 m and 0.5 m roof supports. The same refined support and exact ignition polygon were used within a comparison. Geometry, terrain, and effective material inputs were held fixed between paired options before applying the corresponding clearance actions.

### Fire spread model

We used the PyFireCA V3 thermal cellular automaton on disjoint roof and ground patches. Combustible patches progress through unburned, heating, active burning and burnout states. Non-combustible patches act as inert cells. An eligible patch with fuel ignites when its evolving surface temperature reaches its fuel-class threshold. Each burning patch follows a class-specific heat release curve with quadratic growth, a steady interval and linear decay. At each step, the model integrates this curve over the interval, caps the resulting release by the remaining fuel energy, and updates the remaining fuel mass. The model uses the same state and thermal equations across layouts. Urban fire cellular automata and physics-based spread models provide methodological precedents.［9, 10］

For a burning patch $i$, the incident radiative power at receiver $j$ is the radiative fraction of its interval-mean heat release rate multiplied by a geometric weight $W_{ij}$. For distinct patches, the weight was calculated as

$$
W_{ij} = A_j^{\mathrm{rec}}
   \sum_{p\in i}\sum_{q\in j}
   a_{ip}\,b_{jq}\,
   \mathbf{1}\!\left(\lVert\mathbf{x}_q-\mathbf{x}_p\rVert_{xy}\le R\right)
   \frac{\exp(-k_{\mathrm{a}}d_{pq})}{4\pi(d_{pq}^2+d_0^2)}.
$$

Here $a_{ip}$ and $b_{jq}$ are normalized area weights for source and receiver samples, $A_j^{\mathrm{rec}}$ is receiver area, $k_{\mathrm{a}}=0.001\ \mathrm{m}^{-1}$ is atmospheric attenuation, $d_0=5\ \mathrm{m}$ regularizes the near field, and $R=15\ \mathrm{m}$ is the primary horizontal search radius. The radius is applied to the horizontal sample coordinates before the wind displacement. In the kernel, $d_{pq}$ is the three-dimensional distance from the displaced source to the receiver, which is located at the terrain elevation plus its effective receiver height. For a source with nominal height $h_i$, a uniform wind of speed $U$ tilts the equivalent flame through $\theta=\arctan(k_{\mathrm{w}}U)$, where $k_{\mathrm{w}}=0.2\ \mathrm{s\,m^{-1}}$. This moves the radiation source by $(h_i/2)\sin\theta$ downwind and places it at $(h_i/2)\cos\theta$ above the terrain. The calm case is recovered at $U=0$.

We included flame-contact heat exchange on permitted interfaces between physically adjacent fuel patches. Patches had to lie in the same or face-adjacent parent cells, share positive boundary length and place the receiver height within the source flame interval. Across a common cell face, the vertical contact check used a common local base elevation. Contact exchange used a gas temperature of 1,100 K, a heat transfer coefficient of 50 W m⁻² K⁻¹ and a nominal impingement fraction of 0.5. The exchange was scaled by the source's coverage and thermal activity. Contact fractions were normalized at the receiver and the summed heat supplied by each source was bounded by its non-radiative heat release.

For a susceptible patch $j$, the net heat flux per covered area was

$$
q_{j,\mathrm{net}} =
\frac{\alpha_j P_{j,\mathrm{rad}}+P_{j,\mathrm{contact}}}{A_j}
-\left[h_j(1-f_j)+h_{j,\mathrm{back}}\right](T_j-T_\infty)
-\epsilon_j\sigma\left(T_j^4-T_\infty^4\right).
$$

Here $A_j$ is the covered physical area, $\alpha_j$ is the surface absorptivity, $P_{j,\mathrm{rad}}$ is incident radiative power, $P_{j,\mathrm{contact}}$ is applied contact power, and $f_j$ is the applied contact fraction. The ambient and backing exchange coefficients are $h_j$ and $h_{j,\mathrm{back}}$, and $\epsilon_j$ and $\sigma$ are emissivity and the Stefan–Boltzmann constant. The update accounted for sensible heating of the moist fuel and water evaporation before continuing dry heating. The atmospheric reference temperature $T_\infty$ was 300 K. We limited the solver step to 1 s and bounded it for stability of the explicit surface update. The 60-min comparison used a checkpoint at 3,600 s. Wind modified the radiation geometry. The model ran with no firebrand transport or active suppression.

### Layout construction

We used an uncleared control, two heritage-only rings and two multipoint ring layouts. The heritage-only anchors were the designated roof polygons. The multipoint layouts used the same designated targets plus four retained ordinary roof polygons surrounding the registered historical source locations in each village. Here a historical source is a previously used modelling input. For either anchor set $A$, we formed the band $\mathrm{buffer}(\cup A,w)\setminus\cup A$, with nominal widths $w=2.5$ and $15.01\ \mathrm{m}$. The widths define the simulated interventions. They bracket the primary search radius by design. The layout generator applied clearance to ordinary roof tags with positive area overlap above $10^{-8}\ \mathrm{m}^2$, excluding anchor roofs. It selected full eligible ground cells wherever they intersected the band. We derived the cleared polygons using the ownership rule above and quantified effective roof and ground clearance separately (Table 1).

Two smaller layouts were registered separately using previous design fires. In Pingyan, we started from the narrow local band around the union of the protected and design-source roofs, and added ordinary roof actions and a ground cell selected from the contrast with its wider design comparator. These additions targeted patches that remained as combustible fuel and burned under the narrow design, and were cleared under the wide design, in the design fires. The final fixed layout clears 3,585.57 m². In Batuan, the recorded roof-action ablation taken from the narrow enclosing design retained nine ordinary roof actions and used no ground clearance, giving a final area of 1,089.02 m². Both compact geometries were fixed before evaluation on the held-out panel. We verified that the exported geometry matched the registered roof and ground actions, that the clearances preserved the designated and design-source roofs, and that every selected held-out ignition polygon could be used without modification in both arms (Fig. 4).

### Near-area-matched position control

To isolate a local position comparison, we reused two previous source roofs in each village, one in a drum-tower scenario and one in a bridge scenario. For each source, we compared an uncleared control, the previously selected roof removal, and a position alternative. We drew potential alternatives from ordinary roofs within 15 m of a designated target and assigned each candidate to the nearest target. The alternatives preserved the previously chosen number of roofs assigned to the drum tower and bridge, excluded the registered source roofs, and minimized the absolute difference in nominal removal area among the remaining combinations, with a deterministic identifier tie-break. We recorded the realized clearance separately using the physical polygon union. The 24 calculations (two sites × two sources × three alternatives × two refinement scales) held the physical source polygon, material configuration, and calm conditions constant within each comparison and were evaluated at 20, 40 and 60 min from one trajectory per case. This position test addressed four fixed local cases, with the complete spread endpoint evaluated in the subsequent panels.

### Wind scenarios and held-out ignition design

We applied nine prescribed, spatially uniform and steady conditions to every source, comprising calm and winds of 2 and 4 m s⁻¹ from north, east, south and west relative to grid north. These are unweighted scenario factors. In the first panel, we paired five layouts with four existing design source roofs and four additionally sampled roofs per village, yielding 720 case-layout runs. For the four sampled roofs we selected two in each distance class per village with seed 20261112. Each run was evaluated at 3,600 s and at certified within-domain extinction. A subset using one fixed and one randomly sampled source, calm and 4 m s⁻¹ westerly wind, and each of the five layouts in both villages was re-evaluated to 3,600 s at a 25 m search radius. We held the physical supports, material and geometry fixed in these 40 radius comparisons.

For the main independent panel, the eligibility frame comprised ordinary roofs retained under all five standard layouts with at least 25 m² of eligible unambiguous roof support for the ignition polygon. We removed the eight previous source roofs in each village and stratified the remaining frame by minimum plan distance between the ordinary roof outline and the nearest designated roof outline. The near stratum contained distances of 50 m or less and the far stratum distances greater than 50 m. We included all 23 near roofs in Pingyan and all 20 in Batuan. We selected a simple random sample without replacement of 30 roofs from the 893 eligible far roofs in Pingyan and 30 from 282 in Batuan. The design used PCG64 keyed by SHA-256 with the registered seed 20261113 and site and stratum labels. A separate keyed draw selected one fixed, uniformly sampled location on each selected unambiguous roof. We enlarged a disk centred at that location until its intersection with the unambiguous roof enclosed 25 m². This patch was prescribed to be burning at time zero and served as the identical source across all layouts and wind conditions for that roof. We verified physical patch identity and source energy within each matched comparison.

The 103 roofs yielded 927 roof–wind combinations. All combinations were evaluated under all five standard layouts, giving 4,635 terminal runs. We added 927 terminal runs for the compact layouts, yielding 5,562 distinct runs on this identical held-out panel. The compact comparison paired each new run with the archived multipoint-wide run for the same village, roof and wind condition. We verified the identical ignition patch and matched scenario inputs, and audited the archived closure certificates before reuse. The pairing held the source, wind and observation criteria constant.

### Extinction and spatial coverage

We recorded observations at 3,600 s and at the certified time of extinction. Certification required a completed step after application of scheduled events, zero active burning, no future prescribed ignition or firebrand source, and no susceptible thermally eligible patch at or above its ignition temperature. Under the fixed model inputs and zero future heat release, every susceptible surface had non-positive net heat input and a non-increasing numerical temperature update. The terminal record preserves the actual time and cumulative ignitions of the simulation. We treated any run reaching the original model boundary as a within-domain estimate.

All compact runs used the original model domain. Of the 4,635 archived standard-layout runs, 3,555 used the original domain and 1,080 used computational subdomains. In the latter, an exterior check certified that the ever-ignited support remained farther than $R+5\ \mathrm{m}$ from the artificial crop edge and that omitted exterior cells could neither ignite at ambient temperature nor restrict the integration step to below the adopted bound. Runs that did not meet these checks were repeated on expanded domains. We recorded exposure at the original physical model boundary separately from the crop audit. Input and terminal fingerprints, domain decisions and software cross-checks accompany the case records. Selected replays with the reference solver checked the agreement of the sparse terminal clock and the reference calculations for the recorded comparisons (Supplementary Table 6).

### Outcomes and paired analysis

We defined a heritage ignition event as at least one thermal ignition of a physical patch assigned to any designated roof by a given time. For each designated roof we recorded the first ignition time, the maximum surface temperature rise across its thermally valid patch states and the total absorbed radiative energy summed over its patches. These are metrics of local ignition and exposure in the model. For settlement outcomes we measured burned-out roof area, burned-out ground combustible area and, for the compact comparison, ordinary roof burned-out area $B_R$ separately from designated roofs. The five-layout archive reports all-roof burned-out area, including any burned designated area. Ground clearance $C_G$ was measured as the full cleared footprint, and burned ground $B_G$ as the fuel-covered physical area that burned out. We treated the ground quantities as separate outcomes. For ordinary roofs, the physically cleared union $C_R$ and the burned area of retained patches $B_R$ are disjoint. We therefore computed the total ordinary-roof loss as $L_R=C_R+B_R$. We reported the designation and clearance of roof labels separately from physical loss.

For each village and distance stratum, we calculated paired compact-minus-wide differences on the same roof and wind. We first averaged the nine paired differences within a source roof and then averaged across roofs in that stratum. For a stratum with $n$ sampled roofs among $N$ eligible roofs and roof-level standard deviation $s_d$, the sampling standard error was $s_d\sqrt{(1-n/N)/n}$. Near strata were complete enumerations of their eligible frames and thus contributed zero sampling variance for these prescribed conditions. Wind treatments were kept together within roof. Our case counts are descriptive for the fixed wind set and one registered ignition per roof. The maximum is the largest simulated value observed under a layout within each stratum. We used a $10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m}^2$ numerical tolerance for classifying equal paired area outcomes. For the supplementary affected-roof summary, a roof is affected if any of its nine weather conditions had a heritage ignition. The supplemental one-sided 95% upper bounds for the far stratum used the exact hypergeometric distribution for sampling without replacement. Where we report a village-wide eligible-frame estimate, we use the size of each stratum of the eligible frame as its weight. Computations and summaries used PyFireCA, NumPy and open geospatial libraries.［20, 21］
